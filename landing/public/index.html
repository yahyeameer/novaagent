The problem

A capable agent is not a product a company can buy.

Agents rarely fail to reach production because they aren't smart enough. They stall because nobody can answer four questions with confidence.

01

Who may run what?

Which agent can call which tool, and who on your team is allowed to change that.

02

On whose data?

Customer A's tickets must never be visible to customer B's agents. Not in a view. In the store.

03

With whose approval?

A refund, an outbound email, a contract change. Some actions need a human signature first.

04

Can you prove it?

When the auditor asks what happened last Tuesday at 14:02, you need a record, not a guess.

NOVA is the governance layer that answers all four, enforced inside the runtime, not painted on in a dashboard.

How it works

Declare it once.
The runtime enforces it.

NOVA declares, the Hermes Agent runtime executes. Every change follows one path, in one direction, and leaves a record.

1

Declare your workforce

Describe the organization, agents, policy, knowledge, channels and automations as a small bundle of YAML. No secrets live in it, only the names of variables.

2

Validate and plan

NOVA checks every reference and permission, then shows exactly what applying would change before anything is written. Like a Terraform plan for your AI team.

3

Compile into the runtime

Each agent becomes an isolated runtime profile. Policy compiles into the fail‑closed pre_tool_call hook, so a denied tool is refused inside the agent process.

4

Operate, approve, audit

Your team runs it from the Control Center. Sensitive actions wait for a human. Every write records an intent before it acts and a result after.

The platform

Everything an enterprise deployment needs. Nothing it doesn't.

A small, auditable platform layer on top of a runtime that keeps getting better upstream. NOVA governs what the runtime already does instead of rebuilding it.

Real multi‑tenancy

Every task, run, event, comment and attachment carries a tenant_id, enforced in SQL at the store. Fair scheduling and per‑tenant concurrency caps stop one heavy customer from starving the rest.

Policy that actually refuses

Permissions compile into the runtime's fail‑closed hook. A denied tool is denied inside the agent, not hidden behind a button. Anything that can't be enforced is labelled unenforced instead of quietly claimed.

Write‑ahead audit

Model‑visible means logged. Intent before every action, committed or failed after, with the human actor, tenant and a correlation id.

Human approval gates

Require sign‑off globally or only on customer‑facing channels. "Needs approval on Telegram" is its own compiled policy, not a flag someone can flip.

Governed automations

Scheduled work is a first‑class object with an owner, a reason and permissions. It compiles into the runtime's real cron API through NOVA's validator.

Channels, governed

Telegram, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp and Signal. NOVA owns the routing table and replaces it on every apply, so a revoked connection stops delivering.

White‑label Control Center

React 19 under a strict CSP. Your customer's name, colours and support links come from their bundle. Light and dark, WCAG AA, keyboard‑first.

AWS, with IAM derived

Terraform for EC2, EBS, KMS and CloudWatch. Integration roles are generated from the tenant's declaration and assumed with sts:ExternalId.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is NOVA another agent framework?

No. NOVA is a governance and control layer on top of the Hermes Agent runtime. It doesn't replace the runtime, the scheduler or the task engine. It compiles your declarations into the runtime's own configuration and enforcement hooks, so you keep every upstream improvement.

Where does my data live?

In your own AWS account and region. NOVA is deployed with Terraform onto an EC2 instance with a KMS‑encrypted volume, no inbound ports, and access through SSM Session Manager only. NOVA never writes or reads the values of your credentials, only the names of the variables that hold them.

Which models and channels are supported?

Models are reached through the runtime's providers, including Amazon Bedrock with explicitly enumerated model ids. Channels include Telegram, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp and Signal, routed by the runtime's own gateway and governed by NOVA's routing table.

How do approvals work in practice?

You mark a business action, such as issuing a refund or sending an external email, as requiring approval, either everywhere or only on specific channels. When an agent attempts it, the runtime blocks the task until someone with the right role approves or rejects it in the Control Center. Both the request and the decision are audited.

Can I white‑label it for my clients?

Yes. Product name, colours and support links come from each tenant's bundle and are compiled into the Control Center, so every client sees their own brand.

How long does a deployment take?

A pilot on one workflow typically runs in about 30 days. The deployment itself is a validated Terraform apply; most of the time goes into agreeing the policy and approvals with your team.
